FOUNDATION

PROGRAM PRINCIPLES

Train with purpose.

The program progresses from controlled movement and strength capacity to faster force production and multidirectional landing control.

Weeks 1–3 · Build

Technique, base strength, controlled landings and assisted eccentric work.

Weeks 4–7 · Develop

Heavier compound lifts, more power, unilateral strength and reactive ability.

Weeks 8–10 · Transfer

Maintain strength and emphasize quality jumping, direction changes and control.

The plan is an evidence-informed synthesis, not a clinically validated snowboard-specific injury-prevention protocol. Rest days between the three main sessions are recommended.
WORKOUT EXECUTION

How to use this plan

Start with 5–10 minutes of progressive warm-up (light movement, dynamic mobility and rehearsal sets). Follow the displayed order: perform explosive movements fresh, then strength and supporting work.

Use the checkboxes to track sets completed. Your progress is stored locally in this browser and can be exported.

Rest roughly 2–3 minutes for heavy strength sets, 60–90 seconds for accessory sets and enough time to recover fully between power sets. Keep jump quality high and end a set if landings become uncontrolled.

For unilateral sets, “per side” means completing repetitions on both legs.

RESEARCH & TRANSPARENCY

Built on evidence,
not promises.

What is supported

Lower-limb strength and power measures correlate with competitive snowboard performance. Progressive resistance training reliably develops strength and power in adults. Multicomponent neuromuscular programs reduce some lower-limb injuries in other athletic populations.

Snowboard-specific randomized trials proving this exact off-snow program prevents injuries are lacking.

What is not established

There is no verified percentage by which this program reduces snowboard injury risk. Evidence from soccer, field sports and alpine skiing cannot be assumed to transfer at the same effect size to snowboarding.

For wrist protection, randomized snowboard studies offer more direct support for wrist guards than for any wrist-strengthening exercise.
